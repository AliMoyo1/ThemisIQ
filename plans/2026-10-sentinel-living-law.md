# Sentinel living-law source watch

## Product contract

Sentinel checks pinned official pages for organizations with the corresponding active jurisdiction. An organization chooses every 24 hours, weekly, or every 30 days. The hourly dispatcher only performs checks when each organization's interval is due. Fetches, comparisons, and the first Ask Sentinel retrieval path use **zero AI tokens**. The cadence controls network checks and review volume, not model spending. No user-supplied URL enters the fetcher.

A changed authority page becomes a captured, pending item in Jurisdiction Manager. It does **not** change an operational deadline, approved jurisdiction rule card, RoPA, DPIA, or privacy advice. A DPO verifies its meaning and can approve the capture for extractive retrieval or reject it with a reason. Separately, changing an operational notification clock still requires the versioned rule-card workflow, effective period, source, trigger definition and independent DPO approval.

The page's TLS certificate and host, exact pinned URL, capture time, stored bytes, and SHA-256 hash show where the snapshot came from and whether the stored bytes have changed. They do not prove that the site has legal authority for every entity, that a page edit is an amendment, or that a statute is in force. Review must check publication, commencement, applicability, exceptions, translations and sector rules.

## Data and security path

1. The scheduler acquires a global lease, then enters each active PostgreSQL tenant schema and acquires a per-organization lease. A public source is downloaded at most once in that pass, while each tenant receives its own status and captures. New tables also carry explicit `org_id` for SQLite and defense in depth. The organization-specific interval and last check determine whether a page is due. Failed checks retry after one day and show an error, rather than appearing unchanged.
2. The fetcher permits exact HTTPS URLs from the code-reviewed catalogue, validates normal TLS, refuses redirects and unexpected MIME types, and caps downloaded bytes and extracted text. Pinned PDFs are parsed in the existing networkless, resource-limited evidence extraction child process with page labels. Site content is treated as data, never prompt instructions.
3. Text and raw-byte hashes are computed separately. Identical extracted text records a check but no new version. A changed version preserves compressed source bytes, hash, capture time, old/new text comparison, and a pending state. Only organization DPOs get an in-app notice with the source name; no legal text or personal case detail is emailed.
4. Jurisdiction Manager exposes latest and recent captures, the complete extracted historical text, check errors, source URL, review note and SHA-256 integrity result. Only privacy specialists can change cadence or trigger a manual check. Only DPOs can approve/reject the newest pending capture for retrieval. Each action is audited.
5. Ask Sentinel retrieves from the latest approved capture per source in the requester's active jurisdictions and organization. It verifies the stored source bytes and extracted text before citing them. It returns short exact passages with source URL, capture ID, line or PDF page, time and hash. If no approved relevant passage exists, it says it cannot verify. A newer unapproved capture is flagged. Extractive retrieval sends nothing to an AI provider.
6. A privacy specialist may explicitly request an AI draft. Only the question and up to three verified public excerpts leave the platform, through the organization's AI policy, as `sentinel.ai_law_answer` (`public` and `personal` categories because a free-text question may include personal information). The provider must return a bounded JSON answer and valid numeric citation IDs; a malformed, refused, or unavailable draft falls back to extractive results. AI prose is labelled as unverified for specialist review. No AI output updates a rule or case.
7. An impact preview counts jurisdiction-matched RoPA, DPIA, breach and DSR records visible in the caller's business-unit scope, plus organization rule cards. It is a review queue, not an automatic edit. Pending captures over seven days show as overdue.
8. Each organization can set a 30- to 3650-day review period for captured source copies; the initial period is 2555 days. A DPO can place or release a reasoned legal hold per capture. Due items are marked for disposition review. The system does not automatically erase a source used as legal proof; a separate approved disposal action is needed to preserve audit and legal-hold integrity.

## Follow-on improvements

- Add further gazette and regulator feeds after verifying their current endpoints, terms and publication metadata. Capture instrument, publication date, commencement, repeal, sector and geographic applicability as independently reviewed fields; a changed webpage alone is never an enactment.
- Extend the connected-record preview to owners, policies and controls after their permission and jurisdiction mappings are verified. The existing versioned rule-card workflow already requires an independent DPO approver distinct from its author.
- Introduce a reviewed disposition workflow for copies beyond retention, with legal holds, backup behavior and proof preservation. The current setting is a review trigger, not an automatic purge.
- Improve multilingual retrieval and structured legal propositions. Citation-ID validation binds a draft to displayed excerpts but cannot prove that every generated claim follows from them; specialist legal review remains mandatory.

## Verification and limits

Focused tests cover organization masking, first capture, unchanged page, changed page, human approval, PDF worker routing, shared-fetch fanout, scoped impact counts, legal holds, optional AI citation binding, abstention, disabled jurisdiction, cadence validation and fetch allowlisting. The DPO browser check passed locally, and the PostgreSQL schema test passed against a disposable database for both `public` and a tenant schema. The PR's backend, PostgreSQL and browser jobs remain merge gates. HTTPS and hashing support provenance and storage integrity, not legal authenticity; specialist review is required before relying on a passage. A source site's layout edit can still produce a false-positive change. Local search is lexical retrieval, not a legal reasoning engine.
