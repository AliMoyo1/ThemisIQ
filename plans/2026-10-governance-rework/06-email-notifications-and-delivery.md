# 06. Email notifications and temporary Gmail sender

**Status:** Proposed plan, 2026-10-14. No mailbox, credential, DNS record, route or scheduled job was changed. Read with the [programme index](README.md), [AI Governance workbench](01-ai-governance-specialist-workspace.md), [Vault plan](02-evidence-vault-and-assurance.md), [platform contract](04-platform-truth-navigation-and-reports.md), and [delivery plan](05-delivery-and-acceptance.md).

## 1. Outcome and boundaries

Email should tell the right person that an authorized action is waiting, identify when it is due, and open the source record in ThemisIQ. The in-app action or case remains the source of truth. Mail delivery is a channel, not a second workflow or proof that a deadline was met. A Google mailbox can serve as a temporary, low-volume sender while a managed service identity is prepared. The sender must be dedicated to ThemisIQ, controlled by the organization, monitored for replies/bounces, and removable without changing module business logic.

The first email scope is operational notification to existing internal users. The first AI Governance intake release still uses a customer-owned Google or Microsoft Form, specialist import and human-controlled response export. Do not automatically mail an external requester or put a decision pack in an attachment. External invitations, auditor shares and customer-facing responses have separate recipient, consent, access and approval gates. No marketing or bulk outreach is part of this plan.

No message template can guarantee inbox placement. Delivery depends on authenticated sender identity, provider reputation, volume, recipient interest and domain policy. The template requirements below improve clarity and reduce avoidable filtering; they are not a promise to pass spam filters.

## 3. Recipient and channel contract

An event yields an authorized notification intent, then one or more channel deliveries. Each intent records organization, business unit, source module and record, event/action ID, recipient user ID, role/capability basis, priority, due/trigger time, template key/version, content classification, creation time and a stable deduplication key. A user with several roles gets one message for the same action. Do not BCC unrelated tenants or business units into a shared email. Resolve the current verified address and access at send time; an inactive, deleted, transferred or newly unauthorized user is suppressed with reason. Reassignment creates a new recipient intent and closes the old one.

| Priority | Channel and timing | Repeat/escalation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Critical operational deadline | In-app immediately; email promptly to named owner and permitted backup. Operator sees mail failure. | Recheck source state before each threshold; escalation follows the source module's policy. Email failure invokes an approved alternate contact runbook, not a silent retry until after the deadline. |
| Action required | In-app immediately; email at assignment or a bounded daily digest chosen by role and preference. | One reminder at an agreed threshold; escalate only if still open and policy permits. |
| Informational change | In-app and optional digest. | No repeated instant mail. |
| Management summary | Weekly or agreed schedule from source-backed metrics. | No duplicate module digest for the same audience and period. |
| External recipient | No automatic email in first AI intake release. GRID auditor shares follow a separate approved invitation flow. | Recipient must be specifically authorized; expiry/revocation apply. |

Email preference settings should include immediate action mail, daily digest, weekly summary and quiet hours/timezone. Security or statutory escalations may be mandatory under an organization policy, but the UI must disclose this. Missing email, provider outage, unsubscribed optional digest or access failure must leave the in-app action intact and visible to the responsible administrator. Do not send a summary to a person who could not open its underlying records.

## 5. Template catalogue and examples

Use a shared ThemisIQ wrapper, plain-text alternative and escaped HTML. A template declares key, version, channel category, recipient class, minimum required variables, maximum sensitivity, permitted CTA route and preview examples. The renderer receives structured fields, not arbitrary HTML or free-form AI text. Use a configured HTTPS public app origin and server-generated relative route, validated against an allowlist. Never embed bearer tokens, passwords, raw evidence, DSR identity, breach narrative or report attachments. Display a recognizable sender and a monitored reply address. If replies are not handled in ThemisIQ, say so and provide the real support contact.

| Key and sample subject | Body pattern | Trigger and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `action.assigned.v1`: `ThemisIQ: AI use review assigned` | `An AI use review is assigned to you. Reference: AI-204. Due: 18 Oct 2026. Open the review in ThemisIQ.` | Named reviewer only. Do not include the external request text. |
| `decision.ready.v1`: `ThemisIQ: AI decision ready for review` | `The review brief for AI-204 is ready. Confirm the evidence and record your decision by the due date.` | Eligible independent decider; no approval language before decision. |
| `privacy.deadline.v1`: `ThemisIQ: privacy deadline approaching` | `A privacy case requires attention. Reference: BR-104. Deadline: 14 Oct 2026, 16:00 UTC. Check the jurisdiction and notice decision in Sentinel.` | Named DPO/backup. Use confirmed trigger and approved rule; avoid personal data. |
| `evidence.renew.v1`: `ThemisIQ: evidence review due` | `Evidence for control CT-12 needs review by 20 Oct 2026. Open the current version and record your decision.` | One owner, artifact family and threshold. No raw file or uncontrolled link. |
| `audit.finding.v1`: `ThemisIQ: corrective action assigned` | `A corrective action for audit AU-27 is assigned to you. Due: 23 Oct 2026. Open the finding to review the required response.` | GRID owner, current assignment only. |
| `summary.weekly.v1`: `ThemisIQ: your governance summary for 12 to 18 Oct` | `You have 3 assigned actions due this week and 1 review awaiting decision. Open My Work for details.` | Scoped digest; counts reconcile to click-through, no hidden record leakage. |
| `delivery.failure.v1`: `ThemisIQ: notification delivery needs attention` | `A critical notification could not be accepted by the mail provider. Check the delivery queue and contact runbook.` | Authorized operator. No original sensitive payload in operator email. |

Each body should also show organization/workspace name when authorized, why the recipient received it, source module, current action, expiry of a shared link if applicable, and a help/preference link for optional mail. Keep one clear CTA and a visible text URL to the genuine domain. Do not use all-caps alarm language, misleading urgency, URL shorteners, tracking pixels or unrelated promotional text. Use truthful urgency only when the source clock justifies it. A text-only reader must receive the same actionable information as the HTML reader. For optional digests or subscriptions, include a working preference or unsubscribe route; critical policy-required notices are explained separately.

Current GRID templates use legacy `G.R.I.D AI` branding and a default `localhost` app URL, and the reviewed Evidence scheduler constructs some HTML from unescaped fields with a relative link. Central templates and validated absolute URLs replace these patterns before broad email rollout.

## 6. Durable delivery design

Create a notification-intent/outbox boundary alongside existing `notifications` and `email_reminders`, not a replacement for source actions. A source transaction records the business event and a notification intent or publishes through the existing durable event path. After commit, a worker resolves eligible recipients, renders a versioned template, records one delivery per recipient and channel, and sends through an adapter. Suggested states: `queued`, `sending`, `provider_accepted`, `retry_due`, `permanent_failure`, `suppressed` and `logged_only`. A provider acceptance is not proof of inbox arrival or that a person read it.

Deduplication key: organization + source event/action ID + recipient + channel + template purpose + threshold. For recurring reminders, include the due-date/version so an actual reschedule can generate a new notice. The worker leases a delivery row, retries temporary SMTP failures with backoff and a maximum age, suppresses invalid addresses and role-revoked recipients, and exposes a queue of failed critical items to operators. A timeout after remote acceptance can cause an ambiguous retry, so record the ambiguity and prefer a safe duplicate to a silently lost critical alert. Keep delivery attempts and provider response classes, not raw credentials or whole bodies, in the audit trail.

Do not hold a database transaction open while sending SMTP. Rate-limit by account and organization, prioritize statutory/incident alerts over digests, and postpone low-priority mail when the Gmail quota or reputation is under pressure. A critical-source failure, missing address or mail outage should trigger the in-app alert and a documented alternate contact path. Recheck that the source action is still open before dispatching a delayed reminder.

## 7. Temporary Gmail configuration and later service identity

### Temporary sender gate

1. Determine whether this is a dedicated consumer Gmail or a managed Google Workspace mailbox. Use an organization-controlled account, with 2-Step Verification and restricted administrators. A personal mailbox used for ordinary personal mail is unsuitable for tenant notifications. Choose one visible From address that the Gmail account is authorized to send; do not set `From: noreply@themisiq.net` merely because it looks branded. If a domain alias is used, verify provider authorization and SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment first.
2. Store the app password or other supported credential in a deployment secret readable only by the app service, with rotation and removal procedure. Do not enter it into the current Admin Email Settings DB field until the XOR storage path is replaced or disabled. Do not paste it into chat, source control, logs or diagnostics. Google states app passwords require 2-Step Verification and may be unavailable under some organization/Advanced Protection settings; treat them as a temporary compatibility method, not the target identity model.
3. Bind the sender to `smtp.gmail.com:587` with verified STARTTLS, expected server identity and a validated mailbox address. Test connection separately from real acceptance and mailbox receipt. A `console` result is explicitly not delivery. Keep the currently healthy in-app notification path active while the transport is configured.
4. Set a conservative per-day and per-minute application cap below the account's actual provider limit, with queue visibility. A consumer Gmail account may hit Google's cited 500-per-day threshold; Workspace limits differ. Recheck the account's applicable quota before rollout and send only operational traffic during the pilot.
5. Pilot with internal test recipients across Gmail and a second mailbox provider. Inspect SPF/DKIM/DMARC results, reply handling, text/HTML rendering, spam/junk placement, quota and failure reports. Start with low-volume noncritical notifications, then enable assigned specialist work, then critical alerts only after the alternate contact path is ready.

### Service identity migration

Keep templates and intent/outbox provider independent. The later managed service account or mail provider must present a verified sender domain, controlled credentials or workload identity, documented quotas, bounce feedback, deliverability metrics and rotation. Introduce it as another adapter behind the same interface. Run a staged cutover with test recipients and a comparison of accepted, bounced and suppressed deliveries. Drain or pause the old queue deliberately, switch sender identity, inspect unexpected duplicates, and revoke the Gmail app password. Do not silently change the From identity for an in-flight external invitation or report pack.

## 8. Authentication, deliverability and privacy requirements

- Authenticate the actual sending domain. Google's current sender guidance requires SPF or DKIM for all senders to personal Gmail, with SPF, DKIM and DMARC for bulk senders; Google recommends all three for reliability. Align the visible From domain with the authenticated identity and monitor domain reputation when using a custom domain. Google explicitly says no provider can guarantee spam-filter passage. See [Google's sender guidelines](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/81126?hl=en), reviewed 2026-10-14.
- For the temporary account, use [Google's app-password rules](https://support.google.com/accounts/answer/185833?hl=en) only if permitted for that account. Google recommends Sign in with Google where supported. Use [Google's account limits](https://support.google.com/mail/answer/22839?hl=en-GB) to set a conservative cap; check the Workspace policy separately if applicable.
- Implement RFC-compliant address and header handling, a plain-text MIME part, consistent From/Reply-To, a descriptive subject, one recipient per sensitive message, and no misleading sender or content. No template wording alone overrides poor authentication or account reputation.
- Do not expose requesters, employees, incident subjects, medical/financial details, model prompts, evidence excerpts, secrets or direct access tokens in email or delivery logs. Prefer a reference and authenticated link. Protect the same content from analytics/tracking services.
- No external list of customer addresses is imported for notifications. Source membership and recipient address are verified at send time; correction, transfer and deletion must stop future delivery. Optional digest preferences are honored, and unsubscribe applies where relevant. Legal/privacy review decides retention for delivery metadata and bounced addresses.
- External mail is not a secure archive or a confirmation of human receipt. The source workflow records its own acknowledgement or legal submission evidence. A statutory notice sent to an authority is a separate controlled process with human sign-off and proof of submission.

## 9. Delivery sequence and acceptance

**E0, inventory and decisions:** Determine mailbox type, controlling organization, permitted content classes, current provider settings and active schedules without revealing secrets. Inventory all direct send paths, recipients, legacy templates, tenant scopes, expected message volumes and real alert duties. Approve a sender identity, Reply-To, operational owner and fallback contact. Decide which staff can configure mail and whether a tenant can customize sender/preference policy.

**E1, sender safety and truthful status:** Move credentials away from the XOR settings field, validate sender and HTTPS app origin, add text/plain output, remove fake delivery success from console/no-address paths, stop Sentinel's arbitrary-user DPO fallback, and correct the demo-request function signature. Audit direct GRID/Sentinel send paths before enabling them. Surface provider health and failures without leaking passwords or full subjects.

**E2, outbox and pilot templates:** Start with AI Governance assigned review/decision, Vault evidence renewal and GRID NC assignment. Generate in-app first, then email through one outbox. Add tenant-safe recipient checks, preference policy, deduplication, retry and audit. Replace GRID branding and unsafe/relative links. Pilot on test data and named internal recipients before enabling broad categories.

**E3, urgent cases and digests:** Bring Sentinel breach/DSR and BCM incident/escalation into the same delivery contract after their trigger, clock, owner and alternate contact paths are approved. Consolidate evidence/GRID overlap. Add management summaries only after source-backed metrics and authorization reconcile. Optional external invitations and approved pack notices come later.

**E4, provider cutover:** After the service identity is ready, compare authentication and acceptance, drain queued messages, rotate credentials and retire Gmail without changing source event semantics.

Acceptance examples: a wrong-unit user receives neither an email nor a count of a hidden record; a second event delivery does not duplicate mail; a transfer stops future messages to the prior owner; a missing address stays visible as undelivered; a console fallback never reports sent; a failed breach email does not set a delivered flag; a corrected due date allows a new reminder; a weekly digest count opens exactly the visible scoped actions; a received message has aligned sender authentication, useful plain text and a working HTTPS link; disabling email does not prevent the in-app workflow. During implementation, add focused checks for these scenarios and a manual mail pilot. No application tests were run to write this plan.
